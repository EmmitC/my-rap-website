# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fans and prospective fans of Maze the 28th (rapper/producer/poet, Kampala, Uganda) discovering the artist via social links, streaming platforms, or word of mouth. They're on mobile most of the time, browsing to stream music, check tour dates, buy merch, or book the artist for a show.

## Product Purpose

A personal artist site that acts as the central hub for Maze the 28th: stream the debut album ("Into the Mind of an Overthinker"), browse mixtapes/demos/albums, watch videos, buy merch, see tour dates, and book shows. Success looks like fans leaving with a stronger emotional read on the artist and taking one concrete action (stream, follow, buy, or book).

## Brand Personality

Raw and introspective. The lyrics turn overthinking into self-discovery; merch lines like "I Choose Violence" carry a defiant, in-your-face edge. Streetwear/underground energy over polish — DIY, gritty, indie-hop, not a major-label sheen.

## Anti-references

Avoid an overly mainstream, airbrushed major-label pop-star look — no generic glossy pop-star merch-store polish. (No hard objection to a clean, well-crafted layout otherwise; "no particular concern" was also flagged, so don't over-index on constraints at the expense of just making it look good.)

## Design Principles

1. **Every screen sells identity, not just product.** Merch, tour, and album pages should read as extensions of the artist's voice, not a generic storefront bolted on.
2. **Raw over polished.** Rough textures, high-contrast color blocking, and directness beat soft gradients and safe corporate minimalism.
3. **Dark stage, bright accent.** Near-black backgrounds with yellow/green/red/blue accents (already established) carry the "spotlight in a dark room" feeling — preserve and extend this, don't flatten it.
4. **One clear action per screen.** Every page should make it obvious what to do next (stream, buy, book, follow) without competing CTAs.
5. **Static-site honesty.** No backend exists yet — build real, working interactions (cart, forms, filters) that are honest about their constraints rather than faking a full e-commerce backend.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA target. Because the whole site runs on a near-black background, contrast needs explicit verification per component (muted grays are the most common failure point) rather than assumed from the existing palette. No other specific accessibility needs raised.
